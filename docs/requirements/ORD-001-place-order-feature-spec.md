# Feature Specification: Place Order

**Feature ID:** ORD-001
**Status:** Approved for build
**Owner:** Product, Order Management

## 1. Overview

Meridian Supply sells industrial products to registered business customers. This feature lets a registered customer place an order for products from the catalog.

As a registered customer, I want to place an order so that Meridian Supply records what I ordered and what it will cost, releases eligible orders for fulfillment, and gives me written confirmation of the outcome.

A valid order is accepted in one of two ways:

- A **Placed** order is recorded, released to fulfillment, and confirmed to the customer.
- A **Held for Review** order is recorded, withheld from fulfillment pending manual review, and acknowledged to the customer without promising fulfillment.

An invalid order is rejected and leaves no business trace. Once an order has been recorded, a temporary inability to complete a required downstream or outbound action does not undo the accepted order; the outstanding action remains owed and must be completed as soon as possible.

## 2. Business Requirements

For this feature:

- A **downstream action** is a notification to Meridian Supply's internal fulfillment system so the fulfillment team can act on a Placed order.
- An **outbound action** is a customer-facing email communicating the outcome of the placement request.
- A **rejection** is a business decision that a request is invalid. It is distinct from a temporary system failure that prevents a valid request from being completed.

**BR-1.** Orders may be placed only by existing customers whose status is Active.

**BR-2.** An order must contain at least one line item. Each line item names one catalog product and a whole-number quantity from 1 to 100 inclusive. Product SKU identity is case-insensitive: differently cased representations of the same SKU identify the same catalog product.

**BR-3.** A product may appear on at most one line of an order, regardless of how its SKU is cased in the request.

**BR-4.** The ordering channel supplies product identifiers and quantities, not prices. Unit prices come from the product catalog at the moment of placement.

**BR-5.** The order total is the sum of quantity times catalog unit price across all lines, in USD to two decimal places. An accepted order records its reference, customer, outcome status, placement time, total, and every line's canonical uppercase product identifier, product name, quantity, catalog unit price, and line total.

**BR-6.** An accepted order whose total is $10,000.00 or less has status Placed. It requires one downstream fulfillment notification containing the order reference, customer identifier, placement time, total, and every line's product, quantity, and price so that fulfillment needs nothing further to act. An accepted order whose total is greater than $10,000.00 has status Held for Review and is not released to fulfillment. The caller receives the order reference, total, and outcome status for either accepted outcome.

**BR-7.** Every placement request carries an order reference generated by the ordering channel. A reference identifies one order and only one order. Resubmitting the same reference with identical content returns the original outcome without creating another order or repeating any downstream or outbound action. Resubmitting the same reference with different content is rejected as a conflict and leaves the original order unchanged.

**BR-8.** Each accepted order requires one outbound email to the customer's email address on file. For a Placed order, the email confirms the order reference, every product name, quantity, catalog unit price and line total, and the order total. For a Held for Review order, the email confirms receipt and explains that the order is under review without promising fulfillment. An address supplied through the ordering channel is never used.

**BR-9.** A rejected request returns the specific business reason for rejection and leaves no trace: no order or order line is recorded, no downstream fulfillment notification is made, and no outbound customer email is sent.

**BR-10.** If the order cannot be recorded completely, placement does not complete: the caller receives a temporary-failure outcome, no partial order survives, and no downstream or outbound action occurs. Once the order has been recorded, placement is complete. An inability to perform a required downstream fulfillment notification and/or outbound customer email must not undo the accepted order or turn it into a failed placement. Each required action that could not be completed remains owed and must be completed as soon as possible. An independent required action still proceeds when possible, and an action already completed must not be repeated.

## 3. Product Catalog Used by the Acceptance Examples

The acceptance examples use the following catalog values so that pricing and threshold outcomes are unambiguous:

| SKU | Product Name | Unit Price (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| PROD-100 | Widget | $19.99 |
| PROD-200 | Gadget | $249.50 |
| PROD-300 | Calibration Rig | $2,500.00 |
| PROD-400 | Turbine Blade | $3,499.00 |

In an acceptance example, PROD-9999 represents a product identifier that is not present in the catalog.

## 4. Acceptance Criteria

The criteria below demonstrate the business requirements through concrete examples. Every clause in a Then block is a distinct, verifiable obligation.

### Expected Paths

**AC-01: A valid order is placed (BR-1, BR-2, BR-4, BR-5, BR-6, BR-8)**

Given an existing active customer with an email address on file
When the customer places an order with reference REF-A1 containing 3 x Widget and 2 x Gadget, using any casing for their SKUs
Then the order is recorded with reference REF-A1, the customer's identifier, status Placed, and the date and time of placement
And exactly two order lines are recorded, each carrying the canonical uppercase SKU, product name, quantity, catalog unit price, and line total (Widget: 3 x $19.99 = $59.97; Gadget: 2 x $249.50 = $499.00)
And the recorded order total is $558.97
And fulfillment is notified once with every item required by BR-6
And one confirmation email is sent to the customer's email address on file with every item required by BR-8
And the caller receives a success response carrying the order reference, order total, and status Placed

**AC-02: A high-value order is held for review (BR-1, BR-2, BR-4, BR-5, BR-6, BR-8)**

Given an existing active customer with an email address on file
When the customer places an order with reference REF-A2 containing 3 x Turbine Blade (total $10,497.00)
Then the order and its line are recorded as in AC-01, except the status is Held for Review
And fulfillment is not notified
And one email is sent to the customer's email address on file stating that the order was received and is under review, without promising fulfillment
And the caller receives a success response carrying the order reference, order total, and status Held for Review

### Rejected Paths

For every rejected path: the caller receives a rejection stating the specific reason, no order or order line is recorded, fulfillment is not notified, and no email is sent.

**AC-03: Unknown customer (BR-1, BR-9).** A placement request carrying a customer identifier that does not belong to an existing customer is rejected because the customer does not exist.

**AC-04: Inactive customer (BR-1, BR-9).** A placement request from an existing inactive customer is rejected because the customer is not active.

**AC-05: No line items (BR-2, BR-9).** A placement request from an active customer with an empty line-item list is rejected.

**AC-06: Quantity out of range (BR-2, BR-9).** A placement request from an active customer containing 101 x Widget is rejected. The same applies to a quantity of 0.

**AC-07: Unknown product (BR-2, BR-9).** A placement request from an active customer containing 2 x Widget and 1 x PROD-9999 is rejected in its entirety. Partial acceptance of the valid line is not permitted.

**AC-08: Repeated product (BR-3, BR-9).** A placement request from an active customer containing Widget on two separate lines is rejected, including when one line uses `PROD-100` and the other uses a differently cased representation such as `prod-100`.

### Edge Cases

**AC-09: Total of exactly $10,000.00 is released normally (BR-5, BR-6)**

Given an existing active customer
When the customer places an order with reference REF-A9 containing 4 x Calibration Rig (total exactly $10,000.00)
Then every obligation of AC-01 holds: the status is Placed, fulfillment is notified, and a confirmation email is sent

**AC-10: Idempotent resubmission (BR-7)**

Given a valid order has already been placed with reference REF-A1
When an identical placement request with reference REF-A1 arrives again, including when its product SKUs differ only in casing
Then the caller receives the original success response
And no additional order or order line exists afterward
And fulfillment receives no additional notification
And no additional email is sent

**AC-11: Reused reference with different content (BR-7)**

Given a valid order has already been placed with reference REF-A1
When a placement request with reference REF-A1 arrives carrying different line items
Then the caller receives a rejection identifying the reference conflict
And the original order is unchanged, with no new records, no notification, and no email

**AC-15: Quantity boundaries are inclusive (BR-2)**

Given an existing active customer
When the customer places an order containing a single line whose quantity is exactly 1, or exactly 100
Then the order is accepted and every obligation of AC-01 holds

### Failure Paths

**AC-12: The order cannot be recorded (BR-9, BR-10)**

Given an existing active customer submits an otherwise valid order
When the order cannot be recorded, or fails partway through being recorded
Then the caller receives a temporary-failure response distinct from a business rejection
And no order or order line survives, in whole or in part
And fulfillment is not notified
And no email is sent

**AC-13: The downstream fulfillment action cannot be completed (BR-6, BR-8, BR-10)**

Given an existing active customer places a valid order
When the order is recorded but fulfillment cannot be notified
Then the order remains accepted and recorded with status Placed
And the outstanding downstream fulfillment action remains owed for completion as soon as possible
And the outbound confirmation email is still sent as in AC-01
And the caller receives the successful Placed outcome

**AC-14: The outbound customer communication cannot be completed (BR-6, BR-8, BR-10)**

Given an existing active customer places a valid order
When the order is recorded and fulfillment is notified but the confirmation email cannot be sent
Then the order remains accepted and recorded with status Placed
And the outstanding outbound customer communication remains owed for completion as soon as possible
And the caller receives the successful Placed outcome

## 5. Out of Scope

Payment and billing. Inventory levels and stock reservation. Order amendment and cancellation. The monitoring and reconciliation process that later completes outstanding downstream fulfillment notifications or outbound customer emails, including recipient-side duplicate handling. Customer registration and authentication. Order lookup and history.

## 6. Technical and Non-Functional Requirements

Sections 1 through 5 are business-owned requirements and acceptance criteria. This page begins implementation-owned requirements. The page boundary is intentional so this material can be separated into its own document later without restructuring the business specification.

### Technical Requirements - how the system is built

**TR-1.** Fulfillment is notified by publishing to a message broker. The broker is selected by configuration: RabbitMQ in local development, Azure Service Bus in CI and production. The same code runs against either.

**TR-2.** The confirmation email is sent by calling the email team's HTTP API. That team manages its own queueing behind the endpoint; this system integrates synchronously.

**TR-3.** Broker destinations are provisioned infrastructure. Application code looks them up and never creates them, so a mistyped destination fails loudly instead of silently creating one nothing reads.

**TR-4.** The fulfillment destination has a system-defined default so ordinary environments require no destination configuration. An environment may explicitly override that name when its provisioned topology requires a different destination; connection settings and any such override remain external configuration, and no environment-specific value is checked in.

**TR-5.** A placement makes one round trip to the order store. Existence checks, pricing, totalling and the write happen inside one stored procedure and one transaction, which raises coded errors the data layer translates into domain exceptions. The one exception is a resubmitted reference: deciding what the existing order means under BR-7 is the Manager's job, and costs one further read to fetch the original order.

**TR-6.** BR-7 idempotency is enforced by the data model - a unique constraint on the order reference - not by a read-then-write check, so concurrent duplicate submissions cannot both succeed.

**TR-7.** Downstream and outbound action states are recorded in the same transaction as the order. A required action is born `Pending`, a non-required action is `NotRequired`, and successful performance moves a required action to `Completed` with a completion timestamp. The place-order request that creates the order may attempt those actions; an identical resubmission returns the original outcome without repeating them. A `Pending` action is the durable obligation consumed by the out-of-band recovery process.

**TR-8.** Exceptions are domain-specific and business/technical-classified, each raised from exactly one place, each carrying contextual data keyed `Subject.Datum` so data added at a raise site can never collide with data added later.

**TR-9.** The DomainFacade rejects identifiers that exceed the order-store contract before attempting persistence: order references are at most 50 characters, customer identifiers at most 20 characters, and product SKUs at most 20 characters.

**TR-10.** SKU identity is case-insensitive throughout the domain. Placement-request and placed-order domain values are canonicalized to uppercase, duplicate validation and BR-7 comparison use that canonical identity, and accepted order records and outbound representations carry the canonical uppercase SKU.

**TR-11.** After an order commits, failure to record completion of a required action does not reverse or report failure of the order placement. The action remains `Pending`, and the failure is logged with the order reference and action type for external reconciliation. `Pending` does not prove that the downstream recipient did not accept the request. Monitoring, reconciliation, and recipient duplicate handling are owned outside this operation.

**TR-12.** Expected operational failures are represented by the defined domain exception families and isolated so that an independent required action can still proceed. Unexpected defects are not converted into recoverable operational outcomes: they fail fast under the host's terminal policy, while any durable `Pending` action remains available to the external monitoring and reconciliation process.

**TR-13.** Identifiers travel in a restricted alphabet: order references, customer identifiers, and product SKUs may contain only unaccented letters (A-Z, a-z), digits, and hyphens. The DomainFacade refuses any other character before attempting persistence. This keeps identifier identity single-authority: the domain's exact character comparison (TR-10) and the order store's collation-based comparison can never disagree about whether two identifiers are the same, because every character the two rules judge differently is refused at the front door. (Ratified 2026-08-27 following an independent implementation review.)

### Non-Functional Requirements - qualities the system must have

**NFR-1.** A transient failure of a required downstream or outbound integration must not cost the business or customer the outstanding obligation. The adapter uses the integration library's bounded transport retry or recovery policy when one is provided, without duplicating that policy in the Manager. Application-owned HTTP retries use exponential backoff and jitter for 429, 5xx, and transport failures; a standard integer delay-seconds `Retry-After` instruction is honoured and never undercut. A failure that remains after the applicable bounded policy is exhausted leaves the action pending for later completion.

**NFR-2.** A permanently failing request is not retried. Retrying something that cannot succeed only adds latency to a problem a human must fix.

**NFR-3.** Retrying must not make the customer wait indefinitely. The application-owned retry ladder has a finite attempt count because a `Pending` action (TR-7) carries the obligation onward.

**NFR-4.** A failure the system absorbs is never silent. It is logged where it is absorbed, with everything needed to diagnose it, at a severity reflecting that it will self-heal.

**NFR-5.** Constructing the domain performs no I/O. An unreachable dependency surfaces when it is used, not as a startup crash.

**NFR-6.** Downstream systems are expected to comply with their agreed integration and operational contracts. Meridian handles the documented success and failure responses but does not attempt to anticipate every possible form of downstream misbehavior. For example, the email service is responsible for returning operationally reasonable `Retry-After` values; a value outside that agreed behavior is a downstream defect rather than a condition Meridian must speculatively compensate for.
